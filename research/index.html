Research

Background

Research on biological membranes has a long history at SU, going back at least to Lars Erne­ster (who was rescued from Budapest by Raoul Wallenberg in early 1945). As the first Professor of Biochemistry at SU (appointed in 1967), he built an internationally leading research environment focused on bioenergetics and the respiratory chain enzymes. His successor, Bertil Andersson, added photosynthesis to the research profile, and also recruited one of Sweden’s first bioinformaticians, Gunnar von Heijne, to the Department in 1994. As Director of two Strategic Research Centres funded by the Swedish Foundation for Strategic Research (Stockholm Bioinformatics Center, 2000–2006 and the Center for Biomembrane Research, 2006-2016), Vice Director of SciLifeLab (2009-2015) and Director of the SciLifeLab National Cryo-EM Facility (2015-2020), von Heijne helped develop the Department into a powerhouse of biomembrane research, with a strong focus on membrane protein structural biology.

Overview

CEMERAI is designed around four interconnected scientific themes that connect data generation, modelling, prediction, and validation.

Research strategy

The centre integrates glycoproteomics, proteomics, lipidomics, structural biology, mass spectrometry, molecular dynamics, and AI to move from descriptive molecular catalogues toward predictive, multiscale membrane models.

Themes

Multiscale modelling of erythrocytes and mitochondrial membranes

Combining glycoproteomics, lipidomics, proteomics, structural biology, structural mass spectrometry, and integrative AI models to create testable cell membrane models.

Leads:

Modelling membrane alterations in response to external stress, drugs, or disease

Identifying plasma membrane and mitochondrial remodelling events during pathological, drug-induced, or stress-induced states.

Leads:

Comparative and predictive modelling of diverse cell membranes

Building cross-cell-type models to capture conserved and specialised principles of membrane organisation for diagnostics and rational therapeutic design.

Leads:

Synthetic protein design for diagnostics and therapeutics

Using AI tools to develop synthetic binders against key membrane proteins, including glycoproteins with high specificity.

Leads:

Generation of predictive AI models to improve drug transport into cells

Here we will develop generative and predictive AI models to improve our understanding of drug transport into cells.

Leads:

Centre for Modelling Life at the Membrane with AI

CEMERAI brings together membrane biology, proteomics, glycoproteomics, lipidomics, structural biology, simulations, and AI to build predictive, multiscale models of cell membranes.
